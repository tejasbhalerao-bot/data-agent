Dispatch Deviation — Why Pickup Happens Before or After the Promised Date

Dispatch deviation is measured as DATE(digitised_dispatch_promise) − DATE(pickup_time) in calendar days. A positive value means the courier picked up before the promised dispatch date (Early); negative means the courier was late.

A.2 — Early 1d: The Dispatch Promise Assumes a Courier Lead-Time That Does Not Exist

The digitised dispatch promise is constructed as WH promise date + 1 day, on the assumption that couriers need one day after AWB is printed to collect the order. The data shows this assumption is wrong.

98.7%
of Early 1d orders had AWB printed exactly 1 day before the dispatch promise
98.7%
of Early 1d orders had courier collect the same day as AWB — not 1 day later
92.2%
of On-Time orders also had same-day pickup — same-day courier collection is the universal norm

Proof: AWB timing vs dispatch promise (Early 1d · On-Time · Late 1d)

Early 1d (n=6,207) On-Time (n=43,416) Late 1d (n=5,240)
AWB printed 1d before dispatch promise 98.7% 7.8% 1.5%

Courier picked up 1d after AWB 1.3% 7.8% 51.0%
Mechanism Proven

Courier same-day pickup is constant across all dispatch buckets. The sole determinant of whether an order is Early 1d, On-Time, or Late 1d is when the AWB is printed relative to the dispatch promise. The dispatch promise's 1-day courier buffer is structurally wrong for 98.7% of orders.

Two sub-mechanisms explain why AWB gets printed 1 day early

51.5% of Early 1d — Same courier

Warehouse packed early. 82.6% of same-courier Early 1d orders had the AWB printed before the WH promise. The WH promise was set after the courier cutoff (hence the D+1 dispatch promise), but the warehouse finished packing before the cutoff arrived. Same courier came same day. Dispatch landed a day early.

The remaining 17.3% (WH packed late, same courier) cannot be fully explained without courier cutoff timestamps in the data.

36.6% of Early 1d — Switched courier

Original courier's cutoff was missed; replacement courier had a later cutoff. 50.5% of switched-courier Early 1d orders had the WH packing late (after WH promise), meaning the original courier's cutoff was already gone. A replacement courier with a later same-day cutoff was allocated, came same day, and the order dispatched a day before the digitised promise.

The remaining 49.5% had WH packing early — both factors co-occurred.

The remaining 11.9% of Early 1d orders have a null on either the digitised or shipping delivery partner field and cannot be classified into either sub-mechanism.

A.3 — Late Dispatch: Payment Pending Holds Block AWB Printing

When an order enters payment-pending state, the warehouse cannot print the AWB until payment resolves. No AWB means no courier pickup — dispatch is held regardless of warehouse readiness. We measured this across three dimensions: how many orders in each dispatch group are payment-pending, how payment pending affects the invoice-to-AWB gap, and how much of the total lateness is actually explained by this mechanism.

Payment pending is effectively at baseline for Early (1.8%) and On-Time (4.4%) dispatch. In the Late group it is 10× elevated at 42.5% — but critically, this means 57.5% of late orders are not payment-pending at all. The non-pmt baseline is 0.4h across all buckets — AWB prints within minutes of invoice for a non-pending order. Payment pending orders in the Late buckets sit at 23–27h. The entire delta is pure payment hold.

Payment pending flagged → AWB print blocked (~23h hold) → Courier collection shifts to next day → Late 1d dispatch
What Payment Pending Cleanly Explains

Late 1d — 1,975 payment-pending orders (37.7% of Late 1d). A 23.2h invoice-to-AWB hold (IQR: 17.7–25.7h) means the AWB is not printed until the next calendar day. Couriers collect same-day as AWB print (established universally across all dispatch buckets). Result: dispatch moves exactly one day past the promise. The mechanism is tight and fully accounts for this cohort.

For Early 1d and On-Time, payment resolves fast (0.8h and 2.4h median) — the hold is absorbed within the WH window and does not reach the courier collection step.

Limits of the Explanation

Late 2d (1,218 pmt-pending orders, 68.2%). The median hold is 26.6h, IQR 24.1–28.6h — tightly clustered around exactly 24 hours. A 26.6h hold starting from invoice should push AWB to the next calendar day and pickup one day late, not two. The additional day likely comes from payment resolving late in the evening, pushing AWB past the courier's same-day cut-off. This hypothesis is not yet confirmed — courier cut-off timestamps are not available in the dataset.

Late 3d (96 pmt-pending orders, 26.6%). The median hold here is 22.7h — the same as Late 1d. A 22h hold cannot mechanically cause a 3-day dispatch miss. These orders almost certainly have a second root cause stacked on top of payment pending (e.g. doctor/HA call hold, or a separate invoice pipeline delay).

Overall quantum. Payment pending can account for at most 5.74% of all 57,688 egregious orders (42.5% × 13.5%). The remaining 7.76% of late orders — and all of Late 3d+ — are driven by mechanisms that payment pending alone does not explain.

What the Fix Does Not Address

8.1% of the superset remains late even with payment pending fully eliminated. Late 3d and Late 4d+ barely move (−0.2pp and 0pp). These are driven entirely by other mechanisms — the invoice pipeline delay and HA/doctor call SLA issues identified in A.5 below.

Side effect: 547 currently On-Time pmt orders would shift into Early 1d dispatch if payment resolved at baseline speed (+1.1pp in Early). This is because their invoice creation is early enough that a 0.4h AWB print moves pickup to the day before the dispatch promise. Not a customer harm, but a downstream TAT promise inaccuracy to account for.

A.5 — Hypotheses Tested and Rejected

Rejected H11 — Doctor call timing drives WH deviation

Hypothesis: late doctor calls cause late packing, which causes late dispatch. We tested whether the actual doctor call time (actual_doctor_call_time) correlates with WH deviation across all WH buckets. The doctor call being ≥1 hour late topped out at 6.2% even in the worst WH bucket. This is flat noise — doctor call timing has no meaningful causal relationship with warehouse packing deviation.

Rejected Intra-day WH packing hypothesis for Early 1d

Hypothesis: WH was promised to pack at late evening, but packed in the afternoon, enabling the same courier to pick up early on the same day (intra-day effect). The data disproves this. The mechanism is inter-day: AWB is printed a full calendar day before the dispatch promise in 98.7% of Early 1d orders, and courier behavior is identical across On-Time and Early 1d orders (92–99% same-day pickup in both). No intra-day timing shift is at play.

Revised H16 — HA call SLA drives Late 3d+ dispatch

Hypothesis: DOCTOR_AND_HA_CALL_REQUIRED orders take 3+ days to complete the HA call leg, which has no SLA, causing unbounded dispatch delay. HA-involved orders do appear elevated in Late 3d+ buckets (52–62% vs 44.6% On-Time baseline). However, when we measured the gap between dr_confirm_ts and invoice_create_ts (which proxies HA call duration for Dr+HA orders), the pattern is nearly identical for DOCTOR_CALL_REQUIRED orders that have no HA call:

Dispatch BucketDr+HA median gapDr-only median gapHA call contribution
Late 3d16h12h~4h
Late 6d+170h162h~8h

The HA call adds only ~8h at the margin. The invoice creation step itself — affecting both categories equally — is the real bottleneck. H16 is revised: the invoice pipeline delay (H13) drives Late 3d+, not the HA call SLA specifically. This cohort represents 1.4% of the superset (781 orders) and is not being prioritised.

Section B

Delivery TAT Deviation — Why Transit Time Differs From the Promise

Delivery TAT deviation compares the promised transit window (digitised_delivery_promise − digitised_dispatch_promise in days) against the actual transit (delivery_attempt_time − pickup_time in days). A positive deviation (Early) means the courier delivered faster than the promise; a negative deviation (Late) means the courier took longer.

This metric is independent of dispatch timing — it measures only whether the courier's transit speed matched what was promised.

Critical Finding — Late Cohorts

For same-courier Late 1d–4d orders, the shipping promised TAT is identical to the digitised promised TAT in 84–85% of cases. The shipping layer does not correct the under-estimation at all. Both layers promise a transit time the courier reliably cannot meet — and there is no mechanism that updates this between order placement and shipping.

Switched courier orders tell the same story

Switching couriers does not reliably fix the problem. For Early cohorts, the new courier's shipping TAT still beats actual in a minority of cases only (13.6% for Early 2d switched). For Late cohorts, some replacement couriers promise even longer TATs than the original (Late 3d switched: 9.8% of combos show dig=1d / ship=2d or higher / actual=4d). The lane itself — not the specific courier assigned — is the source of the miscalibration.

B.3 — Root Cause: The Transit TAT Lookup Table Has Stale Values

Both the digitised and shipping delivery promises are derived from a transit TAT lookup — a table of expected transit days keyed by pincode / courier / zone combination. This lookup has not been calibrated against recent actuals. The result is two categories of failure that are mirror images of each other:

34,573

Early cohorts (Early 1d + 2d + 3d). The lookup over-estimates transit for these pincode/courier/zone lanes. For the largest cohort alone (Early 2d, n=25,355), 50.8% of orders were promised a 4-day transit but the courier delivered in 2 days — every single time. The courier is performing fine. The lookup is wrong.

18,777

Late cohorts (Late 1d + 2d + 3d + 4d). The lookup under-estimates transit for these lanes. For Late 2d (n=9,978), 30.3% of orders were promised a 2-day transit but took 4 days. The representative Late 2d same-courier pattern — dig=2d / ship=2d / actual=4d — shows neither the digitised nor shipping layer has updated the estimate in years.

Root Cause — Confirmed Across All 7 Cohorts

The transit TAT lookup table is stale for a significant subset of pincode/courier/zone lanes. It systematically over-estimates transit for some lanes (producing Early cohorts) and under-estimates for others (producing Late cohorts). The shipping layer adds no correction in 84–85% of late same-courier cases. One fix addresses all seven cohorts: recalibrate the transit TAT lookup using rolling actuals per lane.

Fix Directions

Problem Affected orders Fix direction
Dispatch Early 1d — 1-day courier buffer in dispatch promise 6,207 (10.8%) Remove the assumed 1-day courier collection lag from the dispatch promise formula, or retain it only for couriers / pincodes where D+1 pickup is actually the norm (currently <2% of orders).
Dispatch Late 1d & 2d — payment pending holds 7,026 (12.2%) Surface payment-pending status at the dispatch promise stage. Either exclude payment-pending orders from standard TAT commitments, or apply a buffer that reflects the average payment resolution time for this order type.
