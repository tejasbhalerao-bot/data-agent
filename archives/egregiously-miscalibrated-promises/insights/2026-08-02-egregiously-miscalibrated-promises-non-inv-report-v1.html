Why Non-SDD Non-Inventory Orders
Miss Their Delivery Promise

A supply-side calibration problem: SKUs are procured far earlier than the WH promise assumes, causing early dispatch — compounded by a stale transit TAT lookup that independently miscalibrates delivery timing.

Section A

Warehouse Deviation — The Promise Assumes Procurement Time That Does Not Exist

Unlike the Inventory set, the Non-Inventory set has the opposite failure: the warehouse is early in 96.4% of egregious orders. The WH promise assumes Non-Inventory procurement takes hours — but orders are invoiced and ready far before the promise window opens.

A.2 — Why the Warehouse Is Early: The WH Promise Is Built on a Wrong Procurement Assumption

The WH promise assumes the SKU must be sourced after order placement. For this set, that assumption is wrong: the SKU is available and the order is invoiced within hours of placement — far before the promise window opens.

The invoice-based measurement confirms this is not a payment-pending artefact: switching from AWB to invoice_create_ts (fires at order confirmation, before any payment state) makes the early-side distribution more extreme, not less.

Core Finding

In Cohort 40 (WH Early + Dispatch Early + Delivery Early, n=26,134 — 87.7%), zero orders had the invoice created after the WH promise. Procurement and invoicing were complete before the WH promise opened in every single order. The WH promise model does not reflect how Non-Inventory procurement actually works.

A.3 — Payment Pending: A Brake, Not a Driver

In the Inventory set, payment pending drives late dispatch — 68.2% rate in Late 2d. Here, the dynamic is inverted.

Because invoices fire so far before the WH promise, even a multi-hour payment hold typically resolves before the WH promise opens. In the 1.1% of cases where the hold pushes past the promise, it shows up in the AWB measurement. Crucially, delaying an already-early AWB closer to the WH promise often means the order dispatches on time — payment pending acts as an unintentional corrective brake on early dispatch.

Key contrast with Inventory

Payment pending's role is reversed here. In Inventory it drives late dispatch; in Non-Inventory it is a minor brake — the 1.1% of orders where it delays AWB past the WH promise would otherwise dispatch even earlier. Its footprint is also diagnostic: the WH promise is so miscalibrated early that even payment holds rarely push into "late" territory.

Section B

Dispatch Deviation — A Direct Consequence of Warehouse Earliness

Dispatch deviation = DATE(digitised_dispatch_promise) − DATE(pickup_time) in calendar days. Unlike the Inventory set — which has independent dispatch failure modes — dispatch deviation here is almost entirely a mechanical consequence of WH deviation (Section A).

B.1 — Distribution Across Cohorts

54.8%

32.6%
Dispatch Early 2d — courier picks up two calendar days before the dispatch promise (~9,700 orders)
7.5%
On-Time — courier picks up on the promised dispatch date (~2,200 orders)

87.4% have an early dispatch; late dispatch is a negligible tail (<5%) — the inverse of the Inventory set.

B.2 — The Mechanism: AWB Print Date = Pickup Date

Both analyses establish one constant: couriers collect on the same calendar day the AWB is printed. This holds across all dispatch buckets and all courier partners.

Because AWB print date = dispatch date, dispatch deviation is determined entirely by when the AWB is printed — which for Non-Inventory happens far before the WH promise.

WH promise too late → Invoice fires early → AWB printed early → Courier collects same day → Dispatch 1–2d early

The dispatch promise is derived from the WH promise (WH promise date + courier lead time buffer). Both are wrong for the same root reason: when the WH promise is miscalibrated 1–2 days early, so is the dispatch promise — and same-day courier collection ensures the full miscalibration is realised.

Single Lever for Dispatch

Fixing the WH promise is the only intervention needed to correct dispatch deviation. The dispatch model is structurally correct — the WH promise anchor it is built on is wrong. Correct the anchor, and dispatch corrects automatically. A small (<1.1%) late-dispatch tail from payment pending is acceptable.

Section C

Delivery TAT Deviation — An Independent Failure Layer From a Stale Lookup

Delivery TAT deviation = promised transit (digitised_delivery_promise − digitised_dispatch_promise) vs actual transit (delivery_attempt_time − pickup_time) in days. Independent of dispatch timing — it measures only whether courier speed matched the promise.

This is where the "WH miscalibration causes everything" story breaks down. Even if dispatch were fixed, 68.8% of orders would still have a delivery TAT deviation — the courier delivers in a different number of days than either promise layer says.

These are pincode/zone combinations with systematic underperformance that neither lookup has captured.

C.3 — Root Cause: The Same Stale Transit TAT Lookup as Inventory, More Stale in Key Lanes

21,315 Early TAT orders (Early 1d + 2d + 3d+)

Lookup over-estimates transit. The TAT lookup assigns 3–4 days for lanes where couriers consistently deliver in 1–2 days. Neither the digitised nor shipping layer captures the actual lane performance — the shipping layer makes zero correction for the dominant Early 1d lanes.

1,325 Late 3d+ orders

Lookup under-estimates transit. Couriers take 3+ days longer than the lookup predicts on specific hard routes. No correction mechanism exists in either promise layer. The lookup has not been updated with actuals for these lanes.

Root Cause — Confirmed

The transit TAT lookup is stale for Non-Inventory lanes, and more stale than for Inventory. For Inventory, shipping partially corrects via H8 for Early 2d lanes. For Non-Inventory's dominant Early 1d lanes, shipping applies zero correction — 60.8% of same-courier Early 1d orders have identical digitised and shipping TAT. The fix is the same: recalibrate the TAT lookup using rolling actuals per lane, explicitly including Non-Inventory lanes.

Simulation Result — Net Negative for this Segment

Payment pending scope is small: 1,622 orders (5.4%). Baseline gap = 0 days (non-pmt-pending Non-Inventory orders have AWB printed same day as invoice). 190 orders rescued (0.64%) — almost entirely from Late 2d orders where removing the payment delay moves delivery close enough to the promise to exit. 494 orders worsen (1.66%) — payment pending was acting as a brake on Early dispatch; removing it pushes these orders from Early 2d into Early 3d/4d/5d+ (Early 3d: +0.88pp, Early 4d: +0.36pp).

Net direction: this fix is harmful for Non-Inventory at the margin. The system-wide payment-pending resolution that rescues Non-SDD Inventory orders will push Non-Inventory Early orders deeper into egregiousness. The two effects must be weighed together when sizing the operational change.
