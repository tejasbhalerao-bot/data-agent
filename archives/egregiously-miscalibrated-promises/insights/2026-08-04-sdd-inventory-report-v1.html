Section A

Mode 1 — The Warehouse Misses Its SDD Window

Cohorts C59+C60+C61 (72.0%, n=2,847): warehouse is late, delivery is late. Unlike Non-Inventory segments where the WH problem was concentrated in a single extreme bucket, SDD Inventory WH lateness is spread across all magnitude buckets. Median lateness is 4 hours — small in absolute terms, but enough to miss a same-day delivery window.

Together they partially account for it — but roughly 63% of the Late WH group has neither. This is operationally different from the other three segments and requires a different intervention: SDD operational SLA enforcement, not promise recalibration.

Mode 2 verdict. The warehouse did its job. The problem is that these 932 orders carry an SDD same-day promise at digitisation, but the courier delivers in 2–3 days — consistent with a standard non-SDD route. The SDD designation is either wrong at the pincode/route level, or the SDD courier assignment is not being honoured post-dispatch. Either way, this is a TAT model failure: SDD is being promised to customers for routes and orders that the fulfilment chain cannot actually serve same-day.
Section C

WH Processing Type — No Misconfiguration

100% of the 3,955 SDD Inventory egregious orders have wh_processing_type = SDD_INVENTORY. This rules out processing type misconfiguration as a driver of either failure mode.

No misconfiguration. Unlike Non-Inventory segments where a wrong WH processing type model was the root cause, SDD Inventory orders are correctly tagged. The problems are operational (Mode 1) and structural in the SDD promise model (Mode 2).
Section D

Fix Direction

Two failure modes require two separate interventions. Unlike the Non-Inventory segments (one lever each), SDD Inventory needs both an operational and a structural fix.

Mode
Problem
Fix direction
Mode 1
72% · C59+60+61
WH misses SDD processing window. Payment pending (12%) and doctor confirmation (25%) are partial contributors; ~63% have neither. Operational variance across all delay magnitudes.
SDD operational SLA enforcement: enforce WH processing SLAs specific to the SDD window (packing cutoff, doctor sign-off cutoff, payment resolution SLA). A promise recalibration will not fix operational delays.
Mode 2
23.6% · C62+C63
WH ready on time, but SDD courier takes 2–3 days on a 0-day promise. Actual TAT is non-SDD in practice.
SDD eligibility audit: identify pincodes / routes where actual courier TAT is consistently >1 day and remove SDD eligibility flag, or reconfigure to a non-SDD promise. Alternatively, audit courier assignment to confirm SDD-configured couriers are being dispatched.
Contrast with other segments. Non-SDD Non-Inventory and SDD Non-Inventory had a single dominant cohort (>87%) and a single root cause (WH processing time model). Non-SDD Inventory had complex multi-layer failures but each layer was diagnosable to a single mechanism. SDD Inventory is the only segment where two independent mechanisms coexist with comparable weight, and where the primary fix is operational rather than promise-model recalibration.
