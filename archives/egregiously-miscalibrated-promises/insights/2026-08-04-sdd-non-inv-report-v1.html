Section A

Warehouse Deviation — The Promise Assumes Procurement Time That Does Not Exist

The pattern mirrors Non-SDD Non-Inventory exactly: the warehouse is early in 97%+ of egregious orders. The WH promise is set assuming Non-Inventory SKUs need to be sourced after order placement — but the SKU is already available and the order is invoiced far before the promise window opens. The miscalibration here is more extreme than the Non-SDD equivalent: the median WH gap is nearly 35 hours.

A.2 — Why the Warehouse Is Early: The WH Promise Is Built on a Wrong Procurement Assumption

The WH promise for SDD Non-Inventory orders assumes the SKU must be sourced after order placement. For this set, that assumption is wrong: the SKU is already available and the order is invoiced well before the promise window opens.

The invoice-based measurement confirms this is not a payment-pending artefact: switching from AWB to invoice_create_ts (fires at order confirmation, before any payment state) produces a distribution that is virtually identical — the early signal is at invoicing, not AWB printing.

Core Finding

In Cohort 69 (WH Early + Dispatch Early + Delivery Early, n=4,167 — 90.8%), zero orders had the invoice created after the WH promise. Procurement and invoicing were complete before the WH promise opened in every single order. The SDD_NON_INVENTORY WH processing time is set assuming procurement time that does not exist for this SKU category.

Section B

Dispatch Deviation — A Direct Consequence, More Extreme Than Non-SDD Non-Inventory

Dispatch deviation = DATE(digitised_dispatch_promise) − DATE(pickup_time) in calendar days. As in Non-SDD Non-Inventory, dispatch deviation here is a mechanical consequence of WH deviation — the courier picks up on the same calendar day the AWB is printed, so the full WH gap translates directly into early dispatch.

B.2 — The Mechanism: AWB Print Date = Pickup Date

Across all analyses in this project, one constant holds: couriers collect on the same calendar day the AWB is printed. This means dispatch deviation is set entirely by when the AWB is printed — which for SDD Non-Inventory happens 35 hours before the WH promise on median.

WH promise too late → Invoice fires ~35h early → AWB printed ~35h early → Courier collects same day → Dispatch 2–3d early

The dispatch promise is derived from the WH promise. When the WH promise is miscalibrated 2–3 calendar days late, so is the dispatch promise — and same-day courier collection ensures the full miscalibration is realised.

Single Lever for Dispatch

Fixing the WH promise is the only intervention needed to correct dispatch deviation. The dispatch formula is structurally correct — the WH promise anchor it is built on is wrong. Correct the anchor, and dispatch corrects automatically.

Section C

Delivery TAT — Couriers Are Performing; This Is Not an Independent Problem

In Non-SDD Non-Inventory, the delivery TAT was an independent second failure layer — even after correcting for dispatch, 68.8% of orders had a transit TAT mismatch driven by a stale lookup table. This compound layer does not exist for SDD Non-Inventory. The SDD couriers are delivering as promised.

Promised and actual TAT are both 0–1 days. The median digitised_delivery_tat_mins is 0 minutes — the system is promising same-day delivery, and couriers are largely delivering same-day or next-day. The TAT lookup is not stale for SDD lanes.

Key Contrast With Non-SDD Non-Inventory

Non-SDD Non-Inventory had two independent failure layers: (1) WH promise over-padded, (2) transit TAT lookup stale — requiring two separate fixes. SDD Non-Inventory has one failure layer only. SDD couriers are delivering in 0–1 days as promised. The delivery gap of 2–3 days is entirely a downstream consequence of the early dispatch, not a separate miscalibration.

C.2 — How a 2–3 Day Delivery Gap Is Produced From a 0–1 Day TAT

The delivery date gap is DATE(digitised_delivery_promise) − DATE(delivery_attempt_time). For Cohort 69: 92.6% Early 2d, 7.4% Early 3d. This seems inconsistent with a 0–1 day TAT — but the arithmetic is straightforward:

The 2-day gap in the customer's experience is entirely a consequence of the dispatch happening 2 days before the promise, which in turn comes from the WH completing 35 hours before the WH promise. Fix the WH promise and the delivery date corrects automatically.

Section D
