  • Test 3 leads overall — showing ETA only at Summary produces the highest PDP→OP (18.24%) and the largest Cart→Summary lift (+5.58pp vs control), suggesting ETA shown too early creates drop-off before users commit.
  • Test 2 closes best at checkout — full ETA visibility drives the highest Summary→OP (74.57%, +5.49pp), meaning customers who reach Summary with full ETA context are most likely to place an order.
  • All three variants beat control — no ETA treatment degrades conversion; the question is where in the funnel it helps most.
  • Sample size: ~3,000 per variant at PDP, dropping to ~500 at order placement — results are directional until significance is confirmed.

  • Test 2 is the clear RU winner — highest PDP→OP (19.37%) and best checkout close rate (77.31% Summary→OP), making full ETA visibility the right treatment for experienced users.
  • Test 3 gains early but loses late — it achieves the biggest Cart→Summary lift (+5.87pp) but gives it back with the worst Summary→OP (70.36%, −4.86pp vs control), netting below Test 2 overall.
  • Returning users respond better to more ETA, not less — they use delivery date as a planning input, and showing it earlier helps them commit rather than drop off.
  • Sample size: ~2,000 RU per variant at PDP, dropping to ~350 at order placement — this is the most reliable segment in the experiment.
