How to Use the NFC Candidates Map

A quick-reference guide for navigating and interpreting the interactive map.

Overview

What the map shows

The map visualises every pincode in India that currently has demand on Truemeds but is not served by same-day or next-day delivery. These are the pincodes where opening a Nano Fulfilment Centre would unlock hyperlocal serviceability.

Each pincode is coloured by the metric you select — orders, views, conversion rate, or average TAT. The amber markers show the single optimal NFC placement per district: the pincode that maximises covered demand within a 10 km radius.

Controls

What each control does

Layers
Top of left panel
Toggle infrastructure and data layers on or off. SDD Pincodes shows the current same-day network. Fulfilment Centres, Micro FCs, Distribution Centres show existing warehouse locations. Today's Non-SDD Pincodes is the NFC opportunity layer — the coloured polygons or bubbles.
Metric
Middle of left panel
Choose what the colour represents. Orders and Views show absolute July 2026 volume. Ord/Day and Views/Day are the same figures divided by 31 days. Conv % is orders ÷ views. Avg TAT and Med TAT show delivery time — red means slow, green means fast.
Bucket filters
Below the metric selector
Click any legend row to filter the map to only that tier. Click multiple rows to select more than one — within a metric, selections are OR. Switch to a different metric and click there to add a second filter — across metrics, selections are AND. For example: (3 days OR 4 days Avg TAT) AND (184+ views). A "Clear all filters" link appears whenever any filter is active.
Display
Below the bucket filters
Switch between Polygons (filled pincode boundaries — better for geographic coverage) and Bubbles (circles scaled by metric value — better for comparing magnitude across pincodes).
NFC Tier toggles
NFC Candidates section, left panel
Show or hide NFC placement markers by leverage tier. Very High (#1–30) is on by default. Each amber dot marks the optimal placement pincode for one district. Hover over a dot for district rank, views and pincodes covered, and coverage percentage.
Search
Bottom of left panel
Jump the map to any location by Pincode, City, or State. Select the search type first, then type and press Go. The map will zoom to the matching area and highlight it.
Common tasks

Three things you'll want to do

1
Find pincodes with slow TAT and high views
  • Select Avg TAT from the metric selector — the map recolours red (slow) to green (fast).
  • In the legend, click the 4 days and 5+ days rows — the map now shows only slow-TAT pincodes.
  • Switch metric to Views — your TAT filter stays active.
  • Click 184+ in the legend — the map now shows only pincodes that are both slow and high-volume. These are the highest-urgency NFC locations.
2
Explore a specific NFC placement
  • In the NFC Candidates section, toggle on the tier containing the district you want (e.g. Very High #1–30).
  • Zoom into the district on the map — use scroll or pinch to zoom, click-drag to pan.
  • Hover over the amber marker to see: district rank, NFC pincode, how many pincodes and views fall within 10 km, and coverage as a % of district total.
  • Click the marker to pin the tooltip in place.
3
Look up a specific city or state
  • In the Search section, select City or State.
  • Type the name and press Go — the map zooms to that area automatically.
  • Switch to Pincode search to jump to a specific pincode and see its metrics in a popup.
Things to know

Quick tips

Filters persist across metric switches

When you switch metric, any bucket filter you set on a previous metric stays active. The cross-metric indicator in the legend shows you what's still applied. Use "Clear all filters" to reset.

Low-volume pincodes hide at country zoom

Pincodes with fewer than 19 orders are hidden when zoomed out to the full-country view to reduce clutter. Zoom in to a region to see all pincodes regardless of volume.

The map works offline — mostly

All data is embedded in the file. If you lose internet, the pincode data, filters, and NFC markers all still work. Only the basemap tiles (the underlying street map) require a connection.
