Truemeds · Network Strategy · August 2026

Network Redesign:
Nano Fulfilment Centre Site Selection

A methodology brief covering the approach used to identify and rank 513 candidate districts for NFC placement across India.

Objective

Why this exercise exists

Truemeds' current fulfilment network — Distribution Centres, Fulfilment Centres, and Micro FCs — provides same-day and next-day delivery to a defined set of pincodes. Outside this network, a material volume of organic demand exists: pincodes with views and orders in July 2026 that are receiving medicine through standard delivery, with average TATs that underserve their intent.

Nano Fulfilment Centres are a lighter asset class than MFCs — smaller footprint, lower capital requirement, designed to extend SDD/NDD coverage into urban and semi-urban pockets beyond hub reach. This exercise answers a precise question: for each qualifying district in India, which single pincode, if a Nano FC were placed there, would bring the most unserved demand into hyperlocal serviceability?

The output is a ranked, geographically optimised list of 513 candidate districts, each with a specific recommended placement pincode, tiered by strategic leverage and visualised on an interactive map.

Methodology

How the analysis was constructed

  1. 1
    Define the opportunity universe

    All pincodes in India with organic demand on Truemeds in July 2026 — at least one view or order — were identified. From this set, pincodes already served by SDD/NDD infrastructure were removed. The remaining pincodes form the NFC opportunity universe: approximately 15,000 pincodes across 513 districts, representing demand currently fulfilled through slower standard delivery channels.

  2. 2
    Derive the delivery radius empirically

    Rather than assuming a coverage radius, R was reverse-engineered from the existing SDD hub network. For each of 38 active SDD hubs, the haversine distance from the hub's geographic centroid to every pincode it serves was computed. The analysis was then restricted to 28 dense urban hubs — the Thane, Chinchpokli, and Vikhroli archetype — which represent the NFC-relevant reference class. Regional DC-style hubs (overnight journeys, large catchment areas) were excluded as a different operating model. The p75 coverage distance across this cohort was 10 km, adopted as R.

  3. 3
    Score and rank districts on a composite metric

    Each of the 513 qualifying districts was scored by aggregating its opportunity pincodes across three dimensions:

    Views score (50%): Percentile rank of total NFC-opportunity pincode views within the district. Districts with higher aggregate demand intent rank higher.

    Conversion score (25%, inverted): Inverse percentile of average conversion rate. Districts where users browse but do not convert likely experience fulfilment friction — these are treated as higher opportunity, not lower.

    TAT score (25%): Percentile rank of average delivery TAT. Higher TAT districts have more to gain from local infrastructure and are ranked accordingly.

    Scores were computed as percentile ranks and combined as a weighted average to produce a single composite score per district.

  4. 4
    Identify the optimal NFC placement per district

    For each of the 513 districts, every pincode in the district was evaluated as a candidate NFC location. For each candidate, the total views of all NFC-opportunity pincodes within 10 km (haversine) were summed. The pincode that maximises this total is the recommended NFC placement — it captures the most unserved demand within one hub's reach.

    This is the single amber marker shown on the map per district. Each marker's tooltip reports the number of pincodes and views within its 10 km radius, and the coverage percentage relative to total district opportunity.

  5. 5
    Tier districts by leverage

    Districts were grouped into five leverage tiers based on their composite rank, making the 513-district list actionable for prioritisation:

    • #1 – 30 Very High — Visakhapatnam to Allahabad
    • #31 – 60 High — Coimbatore to Koch Bihar
    • #61 – 100 Medium-High
    • #101 – 200 Medium
    • #201 – 513 Remaining

    Each tier has an independent toggle on the map, allowing analysis at any level of the funnel without visual noise from lower-priority districts.
